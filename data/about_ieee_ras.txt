IEEE RAS (Robotics and Automation Society) is a professional society of IEEE dedicated to
the fields of robotics and automation. It supports researchers, engineers, and students
working on topics such as robot design, control systems, automation, AI-driven robotics,
human-robot interaction, and industrial automation.

IEEE RAS operates through a global network of local chapters based at universities and
companies. Student chapters organize workshops, hands-on project sessions, technical talks,
robotics competitions, and networking events for members. Many chapters also run mentorship
programs connecting senior members with newer students.

The Society publishes research through IEEE Xplore, including journals and conference
proceedings, and sponsors major international conferences in robotics and automation.


About RAS
The IEEE Robotics and Automation Society’s objectives are scientific, literary and educational in character. The Society strives for the advancement of the theory and practice of robotics and automation engineering and science and of the allied arts and sciences, and for the maintenance of high professional standards among its members, all in consonance with the Constitution and Bylaws of the IEEE and with special attention to such aims within the Field of Interest of the Society. Our Mission is to foster the development and facilitate the exchange of scientific and technological knowledge in Robotics and Automation that benefits members, the profession and humanity. Our Vision is to be the most recognized and respected global organization in Robotics and Automation.
IEEE Robotics & Automation Society: Executive and Administrative Committee (2025)

RAS strives to advance innovation, education, and fundamental and applied research in robotics and automation. Robotics focuses on systems incorporating sensors and actuators that operate autonomously or semi-autonomously in cooperation with humans. Robotics research emphasizes intelligence and adaptability to cope with unstructured environments. Automation research emphasizes efficiency, productivity, quality, and reliability, focusing on systems that operate autonomously, often in structured environments over extended periods, and on the explicit structuring of such environments.

Governance Overview
From Council to Society, by Antal (Tony) Bejczy

The year 2007 can be considered the 20th year of existence of the IEEE Robotics and Automation Society (RAS). It might be then useful for the younger generation to know how the birth of RAS came about. I was very close to the RAS™s birth because my position as President of the IEEE Robotics and Automation Council (RAC) in 1987 required me to assist in the transition from Council to Society. I prepared the following brief notes based on my 1983-1989 IEEE files.

The initial IEEE Robotics and Automation (R&A) entity, the RAC, was founded in 1984, led by George Saridis, who was elected the first RAC President. The existence of the council was justified by the growing number of R&A papers, tutorial and workshop proposals submitted to the IEEE Control Systems Society (CSS), the Computer Society, and other journals and by the overall growing interest in industrial and R&D-oriented R&A.

Unlike IEEE societies, whose membership is made up of individual IEEE members, the members of IEEE Councils are societies. The eight founding societies of the RAC were Aerospace and Electronic Systems; Circuits and Systems; Components, Hybrids, and Manufacturing Technology; Computers; Control Systems; Industrial Electronics; Industry Applications; and Systems, Man and Cybernetics. Saridis and many others of the original RAC officers were from CSS, where I had previously chaired the CSS R&A Committee. The representatives of the eight sponsoring societies constituted the voting members of the Council Administrative Committee (AdCom), and occasional nonvoting observers or guests participated in RAC meetings. The RAC was established to provide focus for IEEE activities in the R&A field. The Council authorities, procedures, and duties were formulated in a Constitution and Bylaws following IEEE standards and rules. The Council had two meetings per year, chaired by the Council President elected each year by the RAC AdCom.

The RAC initiated and sponsored three major activities and products to help create accessible and measurable data and impressions for R&A interests within IEEE and even beyond:

    IEEE Journal of Robotics and Automation, edited by George Bekey
    IEEE RAC Newsletter (which later became IEEE Robotics and Automation Magazine), edited by Wesley Snyder
    IEEE International Conferences on R&A (ICRA).Annual, starting in Atlanta, GA, in 1984 (under the IEEE Computer Society Technical Committee (TC) on Robotics); then in St. Louis, MO, in 1985; in San Francisco, CA, in 1986; in Raleigh, NC, in 1987; and in Philadelphia, PA, in 1988.

Q:What is IEEE RAS?
A:The IEEE Robotics and Automation Society (IEEE RAS) is a professional organization that supports the development and exchange of scientific knowledge in robotics and automation, including both applied and theoretical issues. It was founded in 1984 as the Robotics and Automation Council and became the IEEE Robotics and Automation Society in 1987.
Q:How do I join the chapter?
A:To join an IEEE Robotics and Automation Society (RAS) chapter:

    Become an IEEE member and add the IEEE Robotics and Automation Society membership to your account. RAS chapters are generally for IEEE RAS members.
    ieee-ras.org
    Find your local chapter using the IEEE RAS chapter directory or interactive chapter map.

Q: Do I need robotics experience to join?
A: No. Most student chapters welcome beginners and provide training through workshops and
mentorship from senior members.

Q: What kind of events does IEEE RAS run?
A: Typical events include technical workshops, guest speaker talks, robotics build
competitions, hackathons, and project showcases at the end of each semester.

Some chapters offer discounts or scholarships for the fee — check with your chapter.

Q: How can I get involved beyond just attending events?
A: Most chapters have sub-teams (technical, events, design, outreach) that members can
join to take on more hands-on responsibility and leadership roles over time.

Do I need robotics experience to join?
    No. You don’t need prior robotics experience to join IEEE RAS. It welcomes students, beginners, researchers, engineers, educators, and professionals interested in robotics or automation. How do I join the chapter?
To join an IEEE Robotics and Automation Society (RAS) chapter:

    Join IEEE and add RAS as a technical-society membership through the IEEE membership website.
    Find a nearby RAS chapter using the RAS chapter directory or interactive community map. Chapters may be local/regional professional chapters or student-branch chapters.
    Contact the chapter or attend one of its events. Chapters commonly hold talks, workshops, competitions, and networking activities.
    If your school or area has no chapter, you can still participate through another nearby chapter—or ask your IEEE student branch or local IEEE section about starting one.
    You generally don’t need a separate application to join an existing chapter; becoming an IEEE RAS member and connecting with the local chapter is the usual route.

What events does the chapter run?
Chapter events vary by location, but an IEEE RAS chapter commonly runs:

    Technical talks and guest lectures from robotics researchers or industry professionals
    Hands-on robotics workshops and tutorials
    Robot-building sessions, demonstrations, and lab visits
    Student projects, hackathons, and robotics competitions
    Networking events and career panels
    Social meetups and collaborations with other IEEE student branches
    Outreach activities introducing robotics to schools or the wider community

RAS specifically encourages members to attend local events hosted by regional and sectional chapters. ieee-ras.org
